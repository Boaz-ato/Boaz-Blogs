With the stabiliser formalism in hand, we can now describe concrete codes compactly. A code is fixed by an Abelian subgroup $S \subseteq P_n$ generated by $n-k$ independent operators, and its codespace $T(S)$ is their joint $+1$ eigenspace, of dimension $2^k$. Below we build a small catalogue, from the repetition codes we have already met to the smallest code that corrects an arbitrary single-qubit error.

Throughout, we drop tensor product symbols when listing generators — writing $ZZI$ for $Z \otimes Z \otimes I$ — since the patterns are easier to read that way.

## The 3-Qubit Repetition Code

The generators are $ZZI$ and $IZZ$.

**Commutativity.** $Z$ commutes with $Z$, and $I$ with everything, so $[ZZI,\, IZZ] = 0$.

**Minimality.** Neither generator lies in the group generated by the other: $\langle IZZ \rangle = \{III,\, IZZ\}$ does not contain $ZZI$, and symmetrically.

**Codespace.** Both generators fix $|000\rangle$ and $|111\rangle$, since each $ZZ$ pair sees an even number of $|1\rangle$s. With $n=3$ and $n-k=2$ generators we encode $k=1$ logical qubit:

$$
T(S) = \mathrm{span}\{|000\rangle,\, |111\rangle\}, \qquad |0\rangle_L = |000\rangle,\quad |1\rangle_L = |111\rangle.
$$

A single bit-flip $X_i$ anticommutes with exactly those generators overlapping qubit $i$, and the resulting syndrome locates the flip. The code thus corrects any single $X$ error. It is blind to phase flips, however: $Z_1$ commutes with both generators yet acts non-trivially on the codespace, so it is a weight-one logical operator and the code has distance $d = 1$.

## The Phase-Flip Variant

Replacing $Z$ with $X$ gives generators $XXI$ and $IXX$. This is the same code in the $|\pm\rangle$ basis, with codespace spanned by $|{+}{+}{+}\rangle$ and $|{-}{-}{-}\rangle$. It corrects a single phase flip and is blind to bit flips.

## The 9-Qubit Shor Code

Shor's construction nests one repetition code inside the other, protecting against both error types at once. Its eight generators are

$$
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
Z & Z & I & I & I & I & I & I & I \\
I & Z & Z & I & I & I & I & I & I \\
I & I & I & Z & Z & I & I & I & I \\
I & I & I & I & Z & Z & I & I & I \\
I & I & I & I & I & I & Z & Z & I \\
I & I & I & I & I & I & I & Z & Z \\
X & X & X & X & X & X & I & I & I \\
I & I & I & X & X & X & X & X & X
\end{array}
$$

The six $Z$-type generators are three independent copies of the bit-flip code, one per block of three qubits. The two $X$-type generators form a phase-flip repetition code across the blocks, with $XXX$ playing the role that a single $X$ played before — consistent with $XXX$ being the logical $X$ of the inner repetition code. The result is a $[\![9,1,3]\!]$ code, correcting an arbitrary single-qubit error.

## The 7-Qubit Steane Code

The Steane code achieves the same protection with two fewer qubits. Its six generators are

$$
\begin{array}{ccccccc}
Z & Z & Z & Z & I & I & I \\
Z & Z & I & I & Z & Z & I \\
Z & I & Z & I & Z & I & Z \\
X & X & X & X & I & I & I \\
X & X & I & I & X & X & I \\
X & I & X & I & X & I & X
\end{array}
$$

The three $Z$-type and three $X$-type rows are each the parity-check matrix of the classical $[7,4,3]$ Hamming code. Within a type, commutativity is immediate. Across types, any $Z$-generator and $X$-generator overlap in an even number of positions, so they commute as well. This gives a $[\![7,1,3]\!]$ code — again correcting an arbitrary single-qubit error, but more economically than Shor's.

## The 5-Qubit Code

Seven is not the minimum. The following four generators define a code on just five qubits:

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}
X & Z & Z & X & I \\
I & X & Z & Z & X \\
X & I & X & Z & Z \\
Z & X & I & X & Z
\end{array}
$$

Each generator is a cyclic shift of the one above it. Unlike Shor and Steane, the generators mix $X$ and $Z$ on the same qubit, so this is not a CSS code. It is a $[\![5,1,3]\!]$ code, and five is the fewest qubits with which a single logical qubit can be protected against an arbitrary single-qubit error.

## Summary

| Code | Parameters | Corrects |
|------|:----------:|----------|
| Repetition (bit-flip) | $[\![3,1,1]\!]$ | one $X$ error |
| Repetition (phase-flip) | $[\![3,1,1]\!]$ | one $Z$ error |
| Shor | $[\![9,1,3]\!]$ | any single-qubit error |
| Steane | $[\![7,1,3]\!]$ | any single-qubit error |
| Five-qubit | $[\![5,1,3]\!]$ | any single-qubit error |
